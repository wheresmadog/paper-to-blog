# Default image style

The design reference is a single frame of a user-selected Instagram reel comparing RAG and “RAG + Jev”: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeMlAWOT1cw/. Use its dark palette, typography, and diagram language; the current output uses one method flowchart without its comparison layout or bottom takeaway. This is a style reference, not scientific evidence for other papers. Runtime access to the reel is unnecessary; reproduce the traits below.

- Portrait 9:16 canvas with near-black background, quiet blue/green ambient gradients, generous outer margins and no dashboard chrome.
- Large, concise headline near the top, with selective green or blue emphasis on the central operation. A small eyebrow establishes the subject.
- The main mechanism occupies most of the central canvas. Use one connected input → operations → output flow, enlarged to occupy the space previously used for results and takeaways. Branches and feedback loops belong to that same chart when scientifically necessary.
- Thin but visible connectors, outlined circular or rounded nodes, restrained line icons, short bright labels, and selective emphasis on the key operation. Use arrowheads and spatial grouping to establish causality and reading order.
- End with the chart’s output and a readable paper citation. No bottom takeaway card, results card, benchmark chart, or detailed experimental numbers. Integrate essential method conditions into the chart itself.

Improve the reference's tiny labels for a static image: at 1080-pixel width use roughly 62–82 px for the headline, 32–42 px for phase headings, 28–34 px for mechanism labels and body, and at least 24 px for method conditions and citation. Allow a multi-line source title. Prefer 5–7 meaningful operations over a crowded transcription of every method detail. The template has no content-specific scientific topology; build that from the paper.

Maintain a clear focal point and consistent gutters. Use neutral white for shared elements, blue for inputs or supporting operations, green for the central mechanism, and amber sparingly for decisions or feedback. Never rely on color alone to communicate the difference. Adapt exact spacing and font sizes to content while preserving legibility and overall canvas proportions.
